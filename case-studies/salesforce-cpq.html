Salesforce CPQ Deployment

Replaced standard Salesforce quoting with a full CPQ + Advanced Approvals deployment across the global sales org — eliminating email-chain approval workflows, enforcing pricing compliance, and accelerating quote-to-cash velocity for a complex, multi-product catalog.

The Problem

A global sales org on standard Salesforce quoting. Manual approvals via email chains. Pricing errors slipping through to contracts.

At a pre-IPO infrastructure software company with a complex multi-product catalog and a global sales org, the quoting process had outgrown its tooling. The company was running on standard Salesforce quoting functionality — a native feature that works fine for simple, single-product deals but breaks down under the pressure of tiered pricing, bundle logic, multi-year discounting, and non-standard commercial terms.

The approval process was worse. There was no system of record for deal approvals — discounts above threshold were routed via email to managers, VP of Sales, and Finance in a chain that could stretch across time zones and take days to complete. Deals were sitting approved-but-not-signed because the commercial process had no automation. And with no enforcement mechanism, pricing exceptions were proliferating: non-standard terms were making it into contracts because there was no system to catch them.

Standard quoting works until it doesn't. The breaking point usually comes when you add your second product, your first multi-year deal structure, or your first sales rep who figures out they can offer discounts that Finance hasn't approved. By the time we deployed CPQ, the error rate on manually-built quotes was significant enough that Finance was doing line-by-line contract reviews before every signature.
  • Standard quoting limitations: no bundle logic, no pricing rules, no guardrails on discounting
  • Manual approval chains: email-based routing across Manager → VP → Finance with no tracking, no escalation, no audit trail
  • Pricing errors: manually-built quotes contained pricing inconsistencies that required Finance review before every deal closed
  • No commercial compliance enforcement: non-standard legal terms appearing in deals without Legal sign-off
  • Slow quote-to-cash: days lost between quote completion and contract execution due to unautomated approval and signature workflows

The Approach

Map the quote-to-cash flow first. Design for rep adoption, not just system compliance.

CPQ deployments fail most often for one of two reasons: the pricing model is too complex for reps to navigate, or the approval rules are so strict that reps work around them. I started the project with a design principle: CPQ should make the right quote the easy quote. If reps have to fight the system to do standard deals, adoption will be shallow and the compliance gains won't materialize.

Before touching configuration, I ran a quote-to-cash mapping session with Sales, Finance, and Legal. The goal was to surface every pricing scenario, every exception case, and every approval trigger that existed in the current process — captured in writing, with business owners, before a single CPQ rule was built. This mapping session took two weeks and surfaced 30+ scenarios that would have become CPQ incidents if discovered post-launch.

For the Advanced Approvals design, I worked backward from the question: what does Finance actually need to approve, vs. what can be auto-approved by rule? The answer was that ~60% of deals could be approved automatically if pricing guardrails were enforced at the quote level. Advanced Approvals would only trigger on genuine exceptions — making the human approval step faster and more meaningful.

The Build

Product catalog, pricing rules, approval matrix, e-signature, and rep enablement — in sequence.

01
Product catalog — Clean data model for a multi-product org
Built the CPQ product catalog from scratch: products, price books, and product options organized into bundles by product line. Enforced naming conventions and SKU structure in coordination with Product and Finance so the catalog remained maintainable after go-live. Migrated historical product data from Salesforce standard objects.
02
Pricing rules — Guardrails at the quote level
Configured CPQ pricing rules to enforce discount floors and ceilings by product line and deal size. Bundle discounting logic for multi-product deals. Multi-year deal proration calculated automatically. Price schedule exceptions (ELAs, ramp deals, pilot pricing) built as configurable product options rather than manual overrides — keeping the quote structured while allowing legitimate flexibility.
03
Advanced Approvals — Automated routing with full audit trail
Built a 4-tier approval matrix: deals within standard pricing parameters auto-approve. Discounts above threshold route to Manager. Discounts above a second threshold or non-standard commercial terms route to VP Sales + Finance. Legal terms exceptions route to Legal. All approvals tracked in Salesforce with timestamps, approver identity, and the specific exception that triggered review.
04
DocuSign integration — Quote to contract in one motion
Integrated DocuSign via the native Salesforce + DocuSign connector. Approved quotes generate contract documents automatically from pre-approved templates. Signature routing configured by deal type. Contract status tracked back to Salesforce Opportunity in real time — Finance and RevOps never had to chase for "did this close?"
05
Rep enablement — Guided quoting and self-service training
Built a CPQ quick-reference guide covering the 10 most common quoting scenarios. Ran 4 live training sessions with the global sales org (split by region and time zone). Created a CPQ "cheat sheet" that lived as a Salesforce utility bar component — accessible inside the quoting flow without leaving the screen.
Advanced Approvals only works if the approval matrix is designed by Finance, not for Finance. The most common CPQ failure mode is an approval structure so detailed that approvers treat it as noise and approve everything reflexively. I worked with VP Sales and the CFO to define the specific scenarios that genuinely required human judgment — and automated everything else.

Key Takeaways

What I'd tell anyone about to start a CPQ deployment.

🗺️ Map every pricing scenario before touching configuration. CPQ implementations that skip the discovery phase always discover edge cases post-launch — in production, with live deals. Two weeks of structured discovery with Sales, Finance, and Legal is the highest-leverage investment in the project.
🎯 Design for the rep, not the administrator. The goal of CPQ is rep adoption, not configuration elegance. If building a quote takes more steps than before, reps will find workarounds. Every UX decision in the quoting flow should be evaluated against: "would a rep in a rush still do this correctly?"
⚖️ Approval matrices should be designed to auto-approve, not to route everything. An approval step that triggers on 80% of deals isn't compliance — it's noise. Work with Finance to define the specific thresholds where human judgment genuinely adds value, then automate everything below that line.
📚 Enablement is a systems deliverable, not an afterthought. CPQ complexity gets absorbed into tribal knowledge without good documentation. The quick-reference guide and in-app cheat sheet reduced support requests by more than anything else in the first 90 days post-launch.
