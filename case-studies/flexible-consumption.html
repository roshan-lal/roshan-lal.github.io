The Problem

A cloud infrastructure software company was launching a cloud product with consumption-based pricing. Every system assumed seat licenses. None of them were ready.

A cloud infrastructure software company had built a successful business on perpetual and subscription seat licenses. The entire GTM stack — Salesforce, CPQ, billing, revenue recognition — was architected around that model. When the business made the strategic decision to launch the cloud platform product with usage-based, consumption billing, every system had to adapt.

This wasn't a minor configuration change. Usage-based billing has fundamentally different data requirements: real-time metering, credit tracking, overage calculation, and ratable revenue recognition — none of which existed in the current stack. And the launch timeline was aggressive: six months from kickoff to revenue.

The stakes were high. This was the company's first cloud-native product and a critical signal to the market about its direction. A systems failure — billing errors, mis-recognized revenue, broken entitlements — would have been highly visible to customers and investors alike.
  • No consumption data model in Salesforce — contracts, entitlements, and usage periods didn't exist as objects
  • No metering pipeline — product usage data lived in engineering systems with no path to CRM or billing
  • CPQ built for fixed-price deals — no quoting capability for credits, commitments, or usage tiers
  • Revenue recognition rules needed to handle ratable recognition across variable consumption periods
  • Cross-functional alignment gap — GTM, Product, Engineering, Finance, and Legal all had different assumptions about how the model would work

The Approach

Form a cross-functional pod. Map the full order-to-revenue flow first. Build in the right sequence.

The first thing I did was map the complete order-to-revenue flow for a consumption model — from a customer signing up, through usage events, through billing, through revenue recognition. Every gap and handoff became a project workstream.

I formed a working pod with representatives from GTM, Product, Engineering, Finance, and PMM — meeting weekly with clear decision rights on each domain. Misalignment at this stage is expensive. We resolved it in rooms before it became code.

The key early decision: extend Salesforce vs. buy a purpose-built billing platform. We chose to extend Salesforce — keeping GTM's single source of truth intact and avoiding a new platform adoption cycle inside a 6-month window. The tradeoff was more complex data modeling. It was the right call.

I sequenced the build around dependencies: data model first, then the metering pipeline, then CPQ quoting flows, then rev rec configuration, then rep enablement. Each layer had to be stable before the next could be built reliably.

The Build

A new Salesforce data model, a real-time usage pipeline, CPQ for consumption, and end-to-end revenue recognition.

01
Salesforce data model — Custom objects for consumption
Built a new object layer in Salesforce: Consumption Contract (top-level), Usage Period (tracks active measurement windows), Entitlement (what the customer purchased), and Credit Balance (remaining usage). Each related to Account and Opportunity.
02
Metering pipeline — Engineering APIs → Snowflake → Salesforce
Partnered with Engineering to expose usage events via API. Built a Snowflake transformation layer to aggregate raw events into billable usage summaries. Nightly sync to Salesforce via Apex batch jobs, with real-time alerting on pipeline failures.
03
Salesforce CPQ — Consumption quote types
Extended CPQ with three new quote types: Credit Pack (upfront commitment), Commitment Tier (volume discount at thresholds), and On-Demand (pay-as-you-go with monthly true-up). Each with its own approval workflow and pricing rules.
04
Revenue recognition — Ratable recognition over consumption periods
Configured rev rec rules for consumption contracts: credit packs recognized ratably over the contract term, on-demand recognized as consumed. Coordinated with Finance to validate against ASC 606 requirements before go-live.
05
Rep enablement — New quoting flows and consumption playbook
Built guided CPQ quoting flow for consumption deals, reducing quote creation time vs. manual. Wrote a consumption contract playbook covering pricing strategy, common objections, and usage-based negotiation tactics.

Key Takeaways

What this project taught me about systems for new business models.

🗺️ Map the full flow before touching a single system. The order-to-revenue mapping exercise was the most valuable week of the project. It surfaced every gap, every handoff, and every assumption mismatch before anyone wrote a line of code or configuration. Do this first, always.
🤝 Business Systems is a cross-functional coordination role, not just a technical one. The hardest parts of this project weren't Salesforce configuration — they were getting Finance, Engineering, and GTM to agree on how consumption should be modeled. Invest in those conversations early.
🏗️ Extend your existing systems before adding new ones. The temptation when launching something new is to buy a purpose-built platform. But adding a net-new billing system in a 6-month timeline, while maintaining a legacy Salesforce environment, creates more risk than it solves. Build on what you have until you genuinely can't.
📐 Build for the second launch, not just the first. The consumption framework we built was deliberately generalized — not just for the first cloud platform product, but for any future product using consumption pricing. That decision paid off immediately when subsequent products launched on the same rails.
